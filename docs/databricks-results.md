# Databricks results

Apprentice writes every board, verdict, hint, and robot move to a session journal, and a Databricks notebook turns those journals into bronze, silver, and gold Delta tables with the same analytics code we run on the laptop. The cloud run and the local report agree exactly on every real number below. We report only real sessions, where the board came from the overhead camera, and only the lesson sample-flat-v1: we left stack-test-v1 out because we changed that lesson mid-hackathon and its sessions mix two different builds. On sample-flat-v1, 4 real sessions ran at the table, and the one session that completed the build passed 4 of its 6 steps on the first try (67%), finishing in 122 seconds with 4 mistakes. All 4 mistakes were blocks placed in the wrong cell, two each on C1 and C3, the two corners of the student's edge. After a wrong-cell mistake, the next try passed 0 of 2 times with the first, gentler hint showing and 2 of 2 times with the second, more specific hint. The camera could not read a block on 2 of 26 stable boards (8%), and the brain waited instead of grading in both cases, so no misread became a wrong verdict. These numbers come from a single completed session, so every rate rests on fewer than 5 attempts and shows how the pipeline works rather than how students perform.
