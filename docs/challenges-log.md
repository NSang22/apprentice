# Challenges log

One line per real struggle: what broke, how we fixed it.

- Camera setup tool (`perception/setup_gui.py`) felt laggy and controls seemed to do nothing. Cause was threefold: DSHOW frame buffering queued stale frames, AprilTag detection ran on the full 1280x720 capture, and the webcam silently ignores `CAP_PROP_EXPOSURE`/`CAP_PROP_GAIN`/`CAP_PROP_AUTO_EXPOSURE` entirely (confirmed by probing `.set()` then reading back the value). Fixed lag with `CAP_PROP_BUFFERSIZE=1` and downscaled tag detection; fixed the "nothing changes" confusion by probing camera capability on startup and flagging unsupported controls in the UI instead of silently no-op'ing.
- Grid alignment couldn't be visually verified: the printed grid lines were light gray (72% gray, 0.6pt) and got washed out by the camera's overexposure (no manual exposure control available on this camera), so there was nothing to compare the computed overlay against. Fixed by reading `Apprentice_AprilTag_Grid.pdf`'s exact vector geometry with PyMuPDF (tag centers, grid bounding box) instead of assuming tag center = grid corner, and regenerating the PDF with thick black (2.5pt) grid lines that survive overexposure.
- The original PDF's printed cell labels used row-letter/column-number ("A1 A2 A3" across one row), the opposite of README section 3's column-letter/row-number convention. Caught by reading the PDF's text spans, not by eye. Software was unaffected (cells are computed geometrically, not read from the page), but the printed sheet would have confused anyone cross-referencing it against the UI. Regenerated the PDF with corrected labels.
- All of the above (`perception/setup_gui.py`, `perception/generate_grid_pdf.py`, the regenerated PDF) got dropped once we found `robot/` already had a working, more accurate camera pipeline (`robot/perception/`, 4.4mm mean placement error) built on the robot-executor branch. Its grid geometry matched ours exactly (independently measured), it never needed to see the printed lines to work, and it solved the overexposed-camera problem by tuning HSV thresholds empirically instead of fighting exposure control, which is the approach that actually works. See `docs/tried-first.md`. The same row/column-letter-number swap bug independently exists in `robot/perception/sheet.py` and `robot/assets/workspace_sheet.json`; see `robot/README.md`'s Open Questions for the team decision still needed there.
- Robot: red landed 19 mm off at B2 three runs in a row, after two-cell moves had landed within 12 mm. We first blamed the cube turning in the hand, then the calibration; both were wrong. Touching the 4 corners of B2 showed the arm calibration was within 2 to 7 mm there, and the saved photo at release showed the cube already 19 mm off while still in the jaws. The common factor: a block beside B2 forced a grip with the wrist rolled about 90 degrees, and the calibration only ever saw roll 0. With the neighbour moved (wrist near 0) red landed 7.7 mm off. The grip planner (`robot/skills.py` `rank_grips`) now ranks grips by wrist roll and checks travel paths while planning. Also: `touch_calibrate --middle` first fitted a jaw-tip length of -40 mm, because with the gripper vertical at every touch the tip length and the table height trade off exactly; the middle fit now keeps the tip length fixed.
- Robot: a stack landed visibly off the edge, and the check said 31 mm. Two causes. Glare cut a corner off the yellow cube's blob, so the bounding-box rotation read 27 degrees for a cube sitting square, and the gripper closed 20 degrees off its faces and twisted it. And the check measured the stacked cube as if it stood on the table; with the camera past the sheet's far edge, that alone added over 20 mm. Fixed with a 3D cube-outline fit for rotation (`robot/perception/cubefit.py`), falling back to square when the fit is poor, and by measuring stacked cubes at their real height. The same stack now measures 10 mm.
- Robot: a 5-step task (cell, beside, stack, unstack, restack) ran cleanly until the end, when the gripper started closing for the trip to its parking spot while still over the new 2-block tower; with the gripper tilted at travel height the swinging jaw dipped low enough to catch the top block and knock the tower over. Fixed in `robot/skills.py`: after a stack release the open jaws rise 40 mm above the tower top before moving, and the gripper now opens or closes only above the block being picked or off the sheet (parking, rest), never during travel.
- Robot: the 40 mm lift after a stack release was not enough: at the end of the next run the arm swung from its parking spot back to rest at the usual 120 mm, the arc passed over the 2-block tower (top at 96 mm), and it knocked the tower over again. Fixed by raising every travel move (between blocks, to park, to rest) to 60 mm above the tallest stack, capped at 190 mm (the highest the arm reaches everywhere); the executor tells the arm the tallest stack after every step.
- Robot: a 3-block tower failed at the top block: it landed ~20 mm off, the retry knocked it off the tower. The logs showed the arm pressing on every placement (the tips sprang down 2.7 to 5.5 mm the moment the gripper opened): the end-of-descent sag fix read "block resting on something" as gravity sag and pushed harder. Place descents now skip the sag fix, the arm holds where the block actually stopped before opening, and stacks release a little higher (2 mm more per block underneath). Retries also learn the arm's error at each stack height and apply it to the re-pick as well as the re-place; correcting only the place had left the re-picked block 17 mm off-centre in the jaws.
- Robot: lowering red into B2 was blocked 16 mm up: purple sat off-cell, 3 mm from B2's footprint, and red's corner caught it. The executor now clears the way first: blocks under 8 mm (edge to edge) from a target, or hemming in a block so no grip fits, are moved to the nearest empty cell, then the step runs. The reference block and later steps' targets are never used or moved.
- The monitor's "show me" clip always played from 0 s instead of its start time. Python's `http.server` ignores HTTP Range requests, and Chromium needs them to seek inside a video, so setting `currentTime` silently failed. Fixed with a small range handler in `ui/serve.py`. Also: headless screenshots with `--virtual-time-budget` run the page clock faster than real time, which hid real-time behavior (reconnect, pass hold); we switched to a DevTools client with real waits.
- Switching to Playskool silicone blocks broke color-per-pixel segmentation two ways. Labeling every pixel by its nearest calibrated color split one block into two blobs (its shaded side read as a different color), which the publisher took as a hand in the cell; and three touching blocks formed one blob where no color reached a 30% share, so nothing split and the whole blob read as a hand. Fixed by finding blobs color-agnostically first (colorful, non-highlight pixels), splitting a blob only when a color covers at least half of a block's calibrated footprint, and weighting lightness at half in color distance (CMC 2:1) so shading never flips a hue.
- The Playskool blocks are big enough that a block in a corner cell always covers part of an AprilTag, so fitting the sheet every frame failed exactly when the board was in use ("GRID NOT FOUND", hands_clear false), and the overexposed camera washed the tags gray on top of that. Since the camera and sheet are taped down, we lock the homography once on an empty board, only check that the tags we can still see sit where the lock says (a hidden tag proves nothing), and find blocks by what changed since the empty board instead of by color masks. Three touching blocks first merged into one region big enough to look like a hand; splitting by color before the hand-size rule fixed it.
- Our own block finder (color masks, then change-from-empty-board regions) kept re-solving problems robot/perception had already solved for the arm: touching blocks split by relative hue, color halos trimmed, a cube outline fit for the true footprint, mid-height parallax correction. Perception now calls segment.detect and cubefit.fit_cube directly and only adds the grid lock, the change gate, the cell naming, and calibrated color. Two lessons from Taher's measurements: under our light orange/yellow and teal/light blue can't be told apart, so only red, purple, yellow, teal are in play; and the robot's CUBE_SIZE_MM is still the 48 mm foam cube, so perception takes --block-mm until the real block is measured.
- Our cell names were the transpose of the printed sheet and the robot (letter = column for us, letter = row on the paper and in robot/perception/sheet.py), so only A1, B2, and C3 meant the same square everywhere, and testers reading the paper saw "wrong" cells. We switched perception, the brain's hint directions, and the UI to the paper's convention (letter = row, A on the arm's edge; number = column) and kept the stored side values ("row3" = the student's edge) so old journals still read correctly.
- 3-high stacks broke the board reading on the real rig. The camera sits only 432 mm above the table, so a 141 mm tower's top leans about half its distance from the image center outward and lands over the neighboring cell: the neighbor read unknown and a sliver of the tower's side read as a hand, so the brain waited forever. Perception read the tower itself correctly (B2 L2 [red, teal, yellow] 0.90). We capped demo stacks at two blocks, put towers in the A row so they lean off the grid, and left an empty cell between towers.
- Perception ran at 3 fps (311 ms/frame) on the Jetson. A profile of logs/board.png on the laptop showed the cell search dominating: it scored 13 alternatives per cell over 2 passes (234 render-and-compare calls). We capped the alternatives at 2-high stacks (9 per cell) and skip the second pass when the first changes no cell, which cut a read from 154 to 82 ms and a full frame from 245 to 138 ms on the laptop with the same board reading.
- A placed block took 3 to 4 s to show in the UI because four waits stacked on top of each other: the perception per-cell vote, perception stack memory, the brain stabilizer, and the UI pass hold. We cut the vote window from 5 to 3 frames (2 of 3 still wins), MEMORY_STABLE_FRAMES from 3 to 2, STABLE_SECONDS from 0.6 to 0.3 s (still at least 2 frames), and PASS_HOLD_MS from 1600 to 800. Unknown still never passes and hands over the grid still give WAITING.
